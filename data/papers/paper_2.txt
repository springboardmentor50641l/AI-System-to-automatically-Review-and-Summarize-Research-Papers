Complex operations in the military now require updated approaches to monitor soldiers’ health and performance. This work offers a Digital Twin (DT) approach for overseeing the health of soldiers, combining simulation with AI applications. The DT focuses on a bio-patch with ECG, body temperature, skin hydration and motion sensors worn on the upper chest. With this bio-patch, soldiers can have their vital signs checked continuously in active combat. The model developed with COMSOL Multiphysics examines the effect of exercise on both temperature and blood circulation in different areas, as well as strain on skeletal muscles, all within their tissues. COMSOL simulation results, as well as data collected during field studies, are used to develop Python machine learning models (Random Forest and LSTM) which foretell fatigue, dehydration and musculoskeletal injuries. Initial testing of the new system found that it was 92.5% accurate in spotting the early influences of heat and reduced the time spent on healing by 20%. The correlation study showed that there was a significant link (p < 0.01) between the predicted fatigue index and subjects’ reported tiredness. This paper proposes a system that combines COMSOL simulation and Python’s analysis tools to build a feedback-powered DT model. Using this approach can help in monitoring soldiers, arranging military missions and making real-time changes in defence operations.
